thesisTitle: "Enhancing the Integrity of Algeria's Social Security System: Addressing Fraudulent Activities through Blockchain Technology"
oneLiner: A Hyperledger Fabric network for Algeria's social security system, with an endorsement policy per transaction.
stack:
  - Hyperledger Fabric
  - Node.js
  - Next.js
  - Hyperledger Caliper
claim: >-
  A permissioned blockchain earns its place here because CNAS, providers and
  pharmacies must endorse each other's transactions.
what: >-
  The thesis asks whether a permissioned blockchain can make Algeria's social
  security transactions harder to falsify. It connects CNAS, healthcare
  providers and pharmacies to one Hyperledger Fabric channel and moves patient
  registration, eligibility, prescriptions and invoice settlement into
  chaincode. A Next.js front end gives each organisation its own section. The
  prototype is a local Fabric v2.5 network: three organisation peers, one
  orderer, one channel and one Node.js chaincode.
hardPart: >-
  The fraud problem is not a missing database. CNAS, providers and pharmacies
  keep separate records and can act on them without the others seeing the
  change. A patient's eligibility can be changed without a coverage review; a
  prescription can be issued without CNAS; an invoice can be settled without a
  matching prescription. The hard part is making each of those actions require
  the right parties before it reaches the ledger, without giving one
  organisation unilateral write authority.
design: >-
  Each organisation runs a peer on mychannel: CNASMSP, HealthcareMSP and
  PharmacyMSP, with one Raft orderer and TLS. The chaincode is a single Node.js
  contract, basic. Endorsement is set per transaction category: patient
  management needs AND('CNAS.peer'); prescription issuance needs
  AND('Healthcare.peer','CNAS.peer'); invoice settlement needs
  AND('Pharmacy.peer','CNAS.peer'). The front end is Next.js with Tailwind CSS,
  split into CNAS, healthcare and pharmacy sections.
proves: >-
  The Caliper run exercised three functions, registerPatient, issuePrescription
  and settleInvoice, at send rates from 100 to 1000 TPS (page 62). It reports
  100% success with zero failures. The report records throughput tracking the
  offered load to about 400 TPS, then plateauing at roughly 80-100 TPS. Average
  latency started at 4-8 s, maximum latency reached 10-14 s at the peak, and
  minimum latency stayed at 1-4 s (page 62). Page 66 records a second summary:
  400-500 TPS, 24,000-30,000 transactions per minute, 4-10 s average latency
  and a fastest path under 4 s. Both summaries come from the same Caliper run
  and are kept here as recorded. The run executed the three workflows without a
  transaction failure under the recorded load.
limits:
  - >-
    Measured on one machine: Ubuntu 24.04.1 LTS under WSL on Windows 11, a
    Ryzen 5 5500U, 16 GB of RAM and LevelDB on each peer. The Caliper figures
    describe that configuration.
  - >-
    Throughput and latency appear as two summaries in the report: page 62
    describes a plateau near 80-100 TPS, while page 66 reports 400-500 TPS and
    24,000-30,000 transactions per minute. The Caliper charts are images.
  - >-
    The Caliper load is synthetic and exercises registerPatient,
    issuePrescription and settleInvoice against seeded records.
  - >-
    The network is one channel with one chaincode and one ordering node; the
    endorsement policy is set per transaction category.
  - >-
    The thesis measures the ledger path; the Next.js front end is the access
    layer for CNAS, healthcare providers and pharmacies.
