updatedAt: "2026-10-23"
claim: >-
  On the 24 answerable questions in the committed eval set, the recorded
  recall@8 is 18/24 (75.0%), and the same report fails three production gates,
  so the eval command exits 6.
problem: >-
  A retrieval answer is easy to make plausible and hard to make checkable. A
  claim can name a passage that was never retrieved, quote text that is not in
  the cited chunk, or survive a question the corpus cannot answer. Without a
  measured refusal path, the system answers anyway.
constraint: >-
  No generator model key exists on the build machine, so answers use a labelled
  extractive fallback. The eval set is 30 questions with 24 answerable, not the
  planned 100, and the corpus PDFs are fetched from a pinned manifest rather
  than committed.
architecture: >-
  Ingest pins page offsets and refuses a PDF whose SHA-256 differs from
  corpus.lock.json. Retrieval is hybrid: embeddings and lexical search fused by
  reciprocal rank, then a cross-encoder rerank. The answer path validates every
  claim against its cited chunk, loads a calibrated refusal threshold at boot,
  and drops claims that fail. The eval harness prints Wilson intervals with
  denominators, records the earliest failed stage per question, and exits
  non-zero when a gate fails.
decisions:
  - title: An uncited claim is dropped, not repaired
    choice: >-
      Validate each claim against the citations in the model context, require a
      quote that is a normalised substring of the cited chunk, and drop the
      claim when either check fails.
    rejected: >-
      Generate first, then attach the nearest retrieved passage to every claim.
      A citation added after generation can point at a passage the claim never
      used.
    cost: >-
      The refusal threshold rejects 19 of 24 answerable questions before
      generation, so the answer rate is 5/24 and the user sees refusals where a
      looser system would guess.
    evidence: >-
      tests/test_citations.py covers the rejection path, and the answer-rate,
      citation-precision and faithfulness gates fail in the committed report.
  - title: The refusal threshold is calibrated, never hand-tuned
    choice: >-
      Load the threshold from eval/calibration.json at boot and keep the
      calibration provenance in the report.
    rejected: >-
      Lower the threshold until the answer-rate gate passes. That would trade a
      visible failing gate for an invisible increase in wrong answers.
    cost: >-
      The provisional threshold was fitted on self-retrieval sentences, so on
      human phrasing it refuses 19 of the 24 answerable questions; a refit is
      owed before the answer rate is meaningful.
    evidence: >-
      tests/test_refusal.py asserts the threshold comes from the calibration
      file, and docs/EVAL.md records the threshold and its fit set.
  - title: The failing eval report is committed
    choice: >-
      Commit eval-report.json with the failing gates, let the command exit 6,
      and label the extractive fallback wherever an answer appears.
    rejected: >-
      Relax the gates or omit the failing metrics so the eval exits 0. A green
      gate that hides the measured quality is worse than a red one.
    cost: >-
      Three production gates stay red until a model key and a calibrated
      threshold exist, and every reader sees the failure.
    evidence: >-
      eval-report.json is the artifact for the recall, refusal and answer-rate
      measurements; tests/test_eval.py checks gate failure, intervals and
      stage attribution.
